## 0. Guardrails (every section)
- Every behavioral claim must point to something checkable: a quote with its timestamp, a count, or a measured rate. No evidence, no claim.
- No typologies or labels in the narrative (not 'you're a Driver'). Describe the move, not the person: 'you closed fast', not 'you're a closer'. (Enneagram, Doshas and AMASE stay ONLY in the appendix, as Jason decided.)
- Personal report: leader only, with no employer-facing judgments and never compared by name to peers.
- 'People who say X tend to...': we have no citation pipeline, so NEVER invent a study or citation. The Drama may carry Jason's required signature-phrase interpretation, but framed as a hedged reading ('This phrase often reads as...'), always right after their own quotes, and paired with an observed effect in THIS conversation where one exists ('When you said X, the next speaker...').

## 1. CHARACTER headline (max 12 words), then 3 numbers
Formulas: Role '[Role-noun] who [signature move].' / Contrast '[Strength], until [moment].' / Number '[Striking number] [unit] that [consequence].' / Quote "'[their exact phrase]': [what it did]." / Arc 'Started [state], finished [state].' Each number gets a plain label and a one-clause meaning ('62% talk time: you held the floor most of the way'), and always a comparison point (their baseline, the session average, or 'the rest of the room', never a named peer). Pick numbers that set up the Drama, not vanity metrics.

## 2. DRAMA: Mirror > Pattern > Twist > Cost-or-gift
Mirror: their exact words in quotes with a timestamp. Pattern: how often and when. Twist: what it did, shown by what the other person did next. Cost-or-gift: name both sides, because every tic is serving them. Stems: "You said '___' [n] times, mostly right after ___." / 'You might already know this about yourself: ___.' / "Here's what's interesting: each time, [other person] ___." / "It's doing a job for you. It buys ___. It also costs ___." / "The one moment it didn't happen was [timestamp], and that's when ___." Use 2-3 verbatim quotes per pattern (3-12 words, keep the fillers), max 2 patterns. The sting goes in the MIDDLE, never in the first sentence or the last line. END the Drama on the exception, the moment they did it well, because the One Thing comes from it. Ratio: one challenge per piece of evidence that they can already do the better thing. Second person, present-tense curiosity, max one 'might' per section.

## 3. Transitions: one sentence per seam, don't reuse, always a forward pull, never 'In summary'
Character>Goal: "That's who showed up. Here's what they came to do." or 'You walked in with one job: ___.' Goal>Challenge: "It didn't go to plan, and not only because of you." or 'Three things stood in the way.' Challenge>Drama: 'The other side made it hard. Some of it was you.' or 'Now the interesting part.' Drama>Resolution: 'So how did it end?' or "Here's where it landed." Resolution>One Thing: 'If you change one thing next time, make it this.' or 'One move would change the most.'

## 4. ONE THING
[Concrete verb] + [specific behavior] + [trigger moment] + [measurable target], in 25 words or fewer, plus a one-line why using their own data. Verbs: pause, ask, name, repeat back, count, stop, wait, say. Banned verbs: be, try, consider, work on, focus on, improve, leverage. The trigger must be a recognizable moment ('when they say but', 'first 30 seconds'). The target must be countable by OUR analysis next session (reuse one Character metric) and set about halfway from the current rate to ideal. Exactly one, with no 'also'. Example: "Ask one question that starts with 'what' before you offer a fix. Target: zero fixes in the first 90 seconds." The 3 supporting action items may stay, but the One Thing must dominate visually.

## 5. Ban list (enforce in the validator; regenerate on a hit)
leverage, synergy, actionable insights, growth mindset, journey, unlock, empower, best practices, communication style, stakeholder alignment, coaching, workshop, soft skills. Barnum or unfalsifiable lines ('you value connection', 'you sometimes doubt yourself'), emotions stated as fact, 'you failed to', 'you should have', 'unfortunately', pass/fail grades, peer comparison by name, sarcasm, empty praise ('great job', 'amazing', 'crushed it'), hedge stacks, exclamation points, emoji.

## 6. Numbers as plot
Give numbers a before and after tied to a moment when the data supports it ('40% for the first five minutes, then 71% after the budget question'). Compute per-segment stats (e.g. thirds of the session) so the model has real before/after numbers. Never let the model invent them. Translate to the physical ('two of every three seconds were yours'). Max 3 numbers in Character and 1 new number per later section. The One Thing's metric reappears as its target. GROUP: treat the chord as a cast list ('who talks to whom, and who nobody answered'), naming the pattern in one line, then showing it.
